How I lead design teams

I mentor by asking, not telling. I serve as a second pair of eyes on every project — catching visual and content mistakes, and questioning whether copy actually matches the design brief before it ships. Rather than hand designers answers, I ask what a challenge isn't, to sharpen what it actually is, and have them distill each problem into a single guiding sentence before we move to solutions. I review front-end code alongside visual work, so craft carries all the way to production — and I keep the team's design materials and tools current, so everyone works from the same source of truth.

I'm also hands-on with what's next: currently mastering Python and learning to build and leverage AI agents and LLMs like Claude and ChatGPT, and embracing the technologies reshaping how design gets done.

About

Over 20 years of creative experience, including a decade leading product and design teams in agile environments. I work across the full design thinking process — from ideation to delivery — partnering with organizations from small businesses to Fortune 500 companies to build user‑centric solutions that drive sales and engagement.

I specialize in high-complexity enterprise systems — the kind where the real design problem isn't the interface, it's making sense of permissions, workflows, and edge cases at scale. That's deliberate: it's where design work compounds across an entire organization, not just a single screen.

I'm drawn to complex challenges and to the teams solving them. I mentor by asking rather than telling — serving as a second pair of eyes on every project, from content and visual craft to the code that ships it — because the best products come from teams, not individuals.
